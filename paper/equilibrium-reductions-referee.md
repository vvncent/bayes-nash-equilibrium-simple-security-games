### Mathematical corrections

| Location | Finding and correction |
|---|---|
| Original book, Chapters 1, 2 and 5 | The introduction inferred UEOPL membership from uniqueness and identified the contraction's fixed point with the game's equilibrium. Membership follows from a specific reduction and a membership theorem. The fixed point selects attack and coverage labels, and a rational LP recovers an equilibrium. A game may have several equilibria. The defender equalizes \(d_jw_j\) on partly covered targets, and a type need only be indifferent among targets it actually mixes over. The definition now requires at least one target. |
| Original book, Corollary 5.9 and its following paragraph; reductions, Chapter 13 | The earlier wording called the destination-bit reduction a TFNP result. I checked the actual witness required by Gärtner et al., Definition 13. On their augmented graph, round down each switch's incoming mass and split it between its labeled exits by ceiling and floor of half. This yields a switching flow to the reached terminal. The manuscripts now give the polynomial witness map, including coincident exits, the destination as origin, and terminal loops. Definition 14 asks for a run profile, which can differ from this certificate. [Primary definition](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07702) |
| Reductions, Appendix C, Lemma C.1 | The reverse P-LCP construction assumes a supplied positive parameter and a strict spectral inequality. That promise implies positive diagonal stability of the original matrix. The new proof constructs the diagonal weights and uses a convex quadratic program to solve the promised LCP directly in polynomial bit time. The \(3\times3\) matrix \(I+2C\), with \(C\) a cyclic permutation, has every principal minor positive and fails the promise for every positive parameter. General P-LCP hardness cannot be inferred from this construction. [Convex quadratic programming theorem](https://doi.org/10.1016/0041-5553(80)90098-1) |
| Reductions, Appendix A and Chapter 2 | The programmable market needs the sum of the lengths of the chains attached to each terminal to compute its capacity. The revised proof derives the contribution of every gate. A minimum gate reading the same good twice now obtains separate copies so its buyer has only one offer on each good. The matching repair LP explicitly bounds its slack. The oracle convention now fixes a polynomial witness bound and a polynomial clock on every admissible transcript, and the compiler returns a basic feasible answer of an original support LP. |
| Reductions, Chapters 10, 11, 13 and 20 | The strict price-order argument applies to the set of **all** goods whose price increased. I corrected that quantifier and spelled out the cut argument. The pinned-price path bound uses ratios of endpoint slopes. The switch construction uses the earlier two-copy topology with a different fee sequence. The two-target stopping-game threshold is also computable by sorting the rational candidates directly. |
| Original book, Chapters 4–7 | The ordered marginal-cost proof of switch alternation now includes both endpoints without referring to nonexistent neighboring intervals. The path-following lower bound only uses the prefix reaching budget \(3/8\) and requires uniqueness of intermediate **coverage**. I also replaced a claimed iteration lower bound with the contraction estimate actually proved, and distinguished the search version of ARRIVAL in the complexity-class diagram. |

## Follow-up on Conitzer’s comment, November 26, 2026 (UTC)

Conitzer’s comment, as quoted by Lin, asked for a full resolution or a clear complexity characterization. He described the reductions as interesting conditional on their correctness. In his later comment on Lin’s manuscript, Conitzer wrote “Incidentally someone else suggested ARRIVAL here” and linked to Kim’s post. [Project chronology](arrival-provenance.md) · [Kim’s post](https://x.com/JeonseongKim/status/2124443473428537685)

**What these manuscripts establish.** The reductions volume gives one-call equivalences among exact BNE, the specified affine matching market, and multiclass affine Wardrop equilibrium on parallel roads. Ordinary ARRIVAL supplies a decision lower reduction. P-LCP supplies a search upper reduction and UEOPL membership. The proofs do not give an oracle-free polynomial bound for arbitrary numbers of types and targets, a reduction of unrestricted P-LCP back to BNE, or UEOPL-hardness. Decision completeness relative to an oracle for BNE has a different quantifier and is identified as such in the opening chapter.

## Corrections and additions

- **Appendix A, price gates.** A generator with threshold \(c_g\) that buys a positive amount at the output implies \(p\le c_g\). The previous proof reversed this weak inequality. At \(p<c_g\) the generator spends its whole mass \(K\), and at \(p>c_g\) it spends zero. The output’s guard, reader bound and capacity force the aggregate generator purchase strictly between zero and \(K\). These two facts force \(p=\max_g c_g\). A single generator with all inputs takes their minimum inside its own threshold. I also made the source buyers’ offers on the final goods explicit and counted the readout buyers in the disturbance allowed for each programmed market.
- **Chapters 10 and 16, sparse markets and games.** The lift now assigns the unused mass of each original buyer to explicit unspent amounts on the copies. This fills the private good exactly, including when a buyer takes the outside option. For the sparse game decoder, the text now proves that every port priced below its path maximum fills, even at price zero. This is needed when summing the individual port inequalities to recover the old capacity.
- **Chapter 13, ARRIVAL.** The text credits Haslebacher’s Lemma 14 for the continuous switch update and fractional-cycle observation and identifies the switching-flow certificate used in decoding. The old summary specified the exit law only up to \(K\). The revision supplies the argument ruling out an input above \(K\) in a network with feedback. The alternative, smaller one-resource construction in Theorem C.1 of Lin’s earlier manuscript is now also identified. The two constructions have different size bounds.
- **Chapter 12, ordered Boolean search.** The affine proof now handles redundant rows explicitly. I added a restricted reduction that had remained in the working notes. If a prefix row reads its own bit, one specified prefix bit, and the final bit, fixing the final bit turns its stationarity constraint into a binary relation. The resulting 2-SAT instances give a fixed point or an ordered crossing. The last row can read every bit. This includes all Boolean updates on three ordered bits.
- **Chapter 17, uniqueness.** I added a direct proof of the connectedness implication used for global uniqueness. A small positive diagonal perturbation of the \(P_0\) matrix gives a continuous inverse for every offset. Two separated compact pieces of the unperturbed zero set would force an inverse path to cross a boundary on which the residual stays bounded away from zero. The section also specifies when choices at a zero-prior type count as different equilibria.
- **Scope and sources.** The abstract distinguishes the earlier market embedding, ARRIVAL reduction and P-LCP upper bound from this manuscript’s single-market converse. Integer margins are specified explicitly in the unit-update theorem. The expander overlay gives a degree compatible with every sufficiently large target count. The reverse P-LCP decoder specifies its spectral hypothesis, and the general stochastic-game reduction specifies its succinct representation.

As a local check on the sign, take one generator with output offer \(4-p\) and a best alternative worth 1. At \(p=2\) it buys its full mass. Its threshold is 3. This directly tests the disputed best-response implication before the market-clearing equation is imposed.
