\chapter{Open problems}\label{ch:open}

\paragraph{Idea.} The results bracket exact BNE between ARRIVAL and $\mathsf{UEOPL}$ and give a
subexponential algorithm. Each of them stops short of something. This chapter lists what is not known,
using only what the preceding chapters establish.

\paragraph{1. Polynomial time.} Can an exact BNE of an explicit Bayesian simple security game be computed
in polynomial time? By Theorem~\ref{thm:mk-main} this is the same question as for exact equilibria of
(M1) markets, and by Corollary~\ref{cor:mk-one-resource} it is enough to answer it for games with one
resource, defender payoffs $0$ and $1$, and nearly uniform priors. A positive answer would put ARRIVAL in
$\mathsf P$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:A}).

\paragraph{2. The gap between ARRIVAL and UEOPL.} Exact BNE is ARRIVAL-hard (Theorem~\ref{thm:A}) and in
$\mathsf{UEOPL}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:ueopl-main}). It is not known to be $\mathsf{UEOPL}$-hard, and ARRIVAL's known lower bounds are CC- and PL-hardness~\cite{manuell21}; nothing in this book bounds BNE search from below beyond ARRIVAL. Hardness for $\mathsf{EOPL}$ ($=\mathsf{CLS}$) under many-one reductions is not a sensible target
(Remark~\ref{rem:ueopl-arrival}).

\paragraph{3. The market and P-LCP.} (M1) and ARRIVAL reduce many-one to P-LCP
(Theorem~\ref{plcp:thm:main}, Corollary~\ref{plcp:cor:arrival}). No reduction in the opposite direction
(from P-LCP to (M1), ARRIVAL or the game) is claimed, and P-LCP is not known to be $\mathsf{UEOPL}$-hard,
so nothing here says that (M1) is $\mathsf{UEOPL}$-complete. For the game, the market route gives only a Turing reduction to P-LCP (Corollary~\ref{plcp:cor:bne}),
but Section~\ref{sec:direct-lcp} gives a direct many-one reduction to one P-matrix LCP.

\paragraph{4. Why the market is not an LP.} When slopes factor as $b_{ij}=\beta_i\gamma_j$, (M1) is one
linear program (Section~\ref{sec:ar-gadget-market}). No reduction of general (M1) markets to an LP or to a
concave generalized flow problem~\cite{vegh14} is known; such a reduction would imply that ARRIVAL is in
$\mathsf P$, which would settle a long-open problem; it would also have to be reconciled with the reduction
of Chapter~\ref{ch:arrival}.

\paragraph{5. Pivoting.} Lemke's algorithm with lexicographic pivoting terminates at an equilibrium on
the unperturbed LCP (Proposition~\ref{plcp:prop:q0}), but no polynomial bound on its number of pivots is
known, neither on $M$ nor on $M+\varepsilon I$. The exponential lower bound of Chapter~\ref{ch:lck}
applies to budget-following methods only, not to pivoting on other formulations.

\paragraph{6. Faster algorithms.} The exponent $\sqrt n$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:sx-main} comes from a generic
block search; ARRIVAL itself is solvable in $2^{O(n^{1/3}\log^{2/3}n)}$~\cite{glm26} by a trade-off
specific to its structure. A faster or polynomial algorithm for the contraction of
Section~\ref{sec:contraction} would have to exploit its special structure: every Jacobian column comes
from explicitly listed attacker rows, each a projection onto a simplex (Remark~\ref{rem:ueopl-nopoly}).
A generic polynomial $\ell_1$ solver would also put ARRIVAL in $\mathsf P$. Known polynomial methods for
$\ell_p$-nonexpansive maps need even $p$~\cite{dfz26}.

\paragraph{7. Smaller statements left open.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item The ``no mass creation'' lemma that would allow terminal capacities $C_T>1$
(Remark~\ref{rem:ar-smallterm}) has been checked exactly for small gadgets only.
\item The embedding of Theorem~\ref{thm:mk-embed} needs slopes $\ge1$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:mk-below-one}); a direct variant for small slopes with a larger scaling constant
$S$ exists only as a sketch that one referee checked by hand (Appendix~\ref{app:sources}).
\item The fixed-dimension algorithm via~\cite[Theorem~40]{fgms18} (Remark~\ref{rem:ueopl-fixeddim})
rests on a theorem whose proof was not checked.
\end{itemize}
